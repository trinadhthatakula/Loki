Loki is a logcat reader for Android. Pick an installed app, start a capture, and Loki collects that application's log output until you stop it — then saves it to a text file you can read, keep or share.

It is built for the moment when something misbehaves and you need to know why: a crash you can't reproduce, a network call that silently fails, an app that drains the battery overnight.

<b>What it does</b>

• Pick any installed app and capture its logcat output
• Watch the log live in a terminal-style view while it runs
• Save a capture to a text file
• Share a saved log straight from the app
• Browse and manage everything you've saved

<b>What you need</b>

Loki reads <i>other</i> applications' logs, which needs android.permission.READ_LOGS. Android classifies that as signature|privileged: no app can ask you for it, and you cannot grant it. That restriction has been in place since Android 4.1, deliberately — a log stream carries other apps' secrets.

So Loki borrows the privilege instead, one of two ways:

• <b>Root</b> — Magisk, KernelSU or APatch. Loki runs its capture through a root shell.
• <b>Shizuku</b> — no root required. Pair Shizuku over wireless debugging or ADB, and Loki uses it until you reboot.

Loki's onboarding will walk you through whichever you have. With neither, Loki can only read its own logs, which is not useful.

Android 9.0 (API 28) or newer.

<b>About your logs</b>

Logcat is not a tidy record of one app's thoughts. It carries access tokens, URLs, account names, file paths, and data belonging to other people who use your device.

Loki never sends a capture anywhere. Files stay in Loki's own storage until you choose to share one. But once you share it — in a bug report, a chat, an issue tracker — it is out of your hands. Read a log before you post it.

<b>Free software</b>

Loki is free and open source under GPL-3.0-or-later. No ads, no trackers, no analytics, no account.

The complete source, the issue tracker and every release are at:

<b>Still early</b>

Loki works, but it is young, and it says so. Expect rough edges. Bug reports are genuinely welcome — and the two most useful things you can put in one are your privilege mode (root or Shizuku) and your Loki version.

Where it's heading: live monitoring, and eventually crash detection.
